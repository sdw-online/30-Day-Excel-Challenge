# Why This Challenge?

There are hundreds of Excel courses out there. Free ones, paid ones, 3-hour ones, 30-hour ones. So why this one?

---

## Most Excel courses have the same problem

You watch a video. You nod along. The instructor types a formula, you see the result, and it makes sense in the moment. Two weeks later, someone asks you to write a VLOOKUP and you're back on Google.

That's not a you problem. That's a design problem. Watching someone else build a spreadsheet doesn't build skill. Building it yourself does. Every single day, with data you can actually touch, with problems that force you to think - not just copy.

That's what this challenge fixes.

---

## What makes this different

<p align="center">
  <img src="../assets/banners/why-daily-practice.svg" width="800" alt="Daily Practice">
</p>

### You use Excel every day for 30 days

Not "watch for 30 days." Build. Each day gives you a fresh dataset, a real scenario, and exercises where you have to figure out the formula yourself before checking the solution. By Day 10, you stop Googling basic functions. By Day 20, you're writing nested formulas without thinking about it. By Day 30, Excel is just something you know.

The research is clear on this: spaced repetition beats massed practice every time. 30 minutes a day for 30 days builds stronger recall than 15 hours in a weekend. Your brain needs sleep between sessions to consolidate what you learnt. This challenge is designed around that.

---

<p align="center">
  <img src="../assets/banners/why-real-data.svg" width="800" alt="Real Data">
</p>

### The data is real (not toy examples)

You won't be working with a 5-row spreadsheet of made-up names for 30 days. Each day has a different dataset designed to feel like actual work:

- **Day 2:** Sales transactions at a retail company - product categories, discount codes, revenue calculations
- **Day 7:** Logistics tracking data with messy entries - duplicates, inconsistent formats, missing values from a system export
- **Day 12:** Student performance across 4 schools - benchmarking, conditional summaries, multi-sheet analysis
- **Day 21:** SaaS subscription data - churn analysis, cohort tracking, business metrics
- **Day 30:** Financial operations workbook - multiple sheets, pivot tables, full capstone

When you land a data role, nothing feels unfamiliar because you've already worked with data that looks like this.

---

<p align="center">

### Every exercise puts you in a real role

You're not answering "write a SUM formula." You're a junior analyst whose manager needs a summary report before a client meeting. You're an operations coordinator whose team lead needs the backlog cleaned before standup. You're a finance analyst building a tracker for the operations director.

The scenarios are modelled on real work across logistics, finance, education, healthcare, and SaaS. The pressure is realistic. The questions are the kind your manager would actually ask.

This matters because interviews test problem-solving, not formula recall. If you've spent 30 days solving realistic problems, you walk into an interview with stories to tell.

---

## What you'll walk away with

By the end of 30 days, you'll be able to:

- Build complex workbooks from scratch without Googling every function
- Clean messy data, handle blanks, and transform columns confidently
- Use VLOOKUP, XLOOKUP, and INDEX-MATCH to connect data across sheets
- Write IF, IFS, and nested logic formulas to answer conditional questions
- Build pivot tables and charts that surface real insights
- Design a full reporting workbook from raw data to polished output

More importantly, you'll think differently. Excel teaches you to structure messy reality into clean, answerable questions. That skill doesn't go away.
